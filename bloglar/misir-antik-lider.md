Egypt, one of the first great civilizations in world history, was a civilization spread across northeastern Africa, around the point where the Nile River meets the sea. With a history spanning approximately 7,000 years, Egypt has been ruled by many countries, tribes, and individuals. Egypt has two strong points: its mystical and ancient history dating back to the Pharaohs' era; and its deep and spiritual aspect shaped by Islamic history. In my view as a diver, Egypt's other significance lies in its unparalleled underwater riches along the Red Sea coast. Egyptian diving spots are among the most beautiful diving locations in the world and are the stuff of every diver's dreams.

Egypt is geographically divided into Lower and Upper Egypt. Its economy is based on tourism and textile exports. The alluvial soils along the banks of the Nile River, the world's longest river, produce some of the world's finest long-staple cotton (giza) and sugarcane, which are exported worldwide.

Ancient Egyptians did not value life on Earth; they believed in eternal life after death and prepared for their afterlife during their lifetime. Those with money had special Book of the Dead prepared for them. This book was considered a handbook, a guarantee, for their transition to the afterlife. The Book of the Dead is still considered one of the first sacred books today. They also prepared and decorated their burial sites, believing that this was where they would enter eternal life.

They were mummified after death. During the mummification process, they were laid on a slightly inclined stone. The nasal cartilage was broken, and the brain was extracted through the nasal passage. Linen was stuffed into the eyes through the same passage to prevent them from falling out. Four separate organs were removed intact through small holes made in the sides of the body, placed in jars, and buried near the mummy for future use. The only organ not removed was the heart, which remained in the body to be accounted for on the day of judgment.

The person performing the mummification wore a jackal mask. The jackal, a scavenging animal, was their Mummy God. The mummification process took 40 days. The number 40, a significant number in many civilizations, appears to have been important to the pharaohs as well. During this process, a liquid, the formula of which remains a secret, was applied to the body to prevent the flesh from decaying. Finally, the body was wrapped in linen and placed inside a chest.

If we divide ancient Egyptian history into three periods; the pharaohs built the most impressive pyramids, such as those at Saqqara and Giza, during the Old Kingdom (3000 BC - 2000 BC); smaller and simpler pyramids, such as El Fayoum and Abu Sir, during the Middle Kingdom (2000 BC - 1700 BC); and the rock tombs at Luxor during the New Kingdom (1700 BC - 1100 BC). All of these structures were places where the dead would live in the afterlife. They did not build pyramids in Luxor because they had seen how the pyramids where their ancestors were buried had been plundered, and the land was not suitable for pyramid construction. Therefore, when the pharaohs' tombs in the Valley of the Kings were being built, the workers who went to work were blindfolded, they did not know where they were working, and the burial sites were kept secret.

Ancient Egypt and its Gods

Gods were very important to the ancient Egyptians. They believed that the wise god Osiris descended from the heavens to Egypt. Osiris would rule with his sister and wife, Isis. He brought culture to his people, implemented laws, and taught agriculture. He left Isis to rule the world while he himself traveled the world, teaching these skills to other nations. However, the god Osiris was killed by his brother Seth, who was jealous of his popularity. To pass into the afterlife, the body needed to be intact, but Seth dismembered his brother's body and scattered the pieces throughout the land. Isis began searching for her husband's remains, but one piece was eaten by fish, making reunion with her husband impossible even in the afterlife. However, she found his sperm and gave birth to their son, Horus. Horus grew up and avenged his father by killing his uncle Seth in a fight. However, Seth tore out Horus's eye during the battle. Thoth put the pieces back together and, through magic, restored the eye to its former state. The eyes of Horus also came to represent the moon and the sun above the world, becoming, in a spiritual sense, the never-closing eye of conscience. The evil eye beads we use today are based on this belief of the Ancient Egyptians.

Do I need a visa to enter Egypt?

Turkish citizens holding diplomatic, service, and special passports are exempt from Egyptian visa requirements for visits not exceeding 90 days. Turkish citizens holding ordinary passports are required to obtain an Egyptian visa.

As of May 2023, individuals traveling to Egypt can obtain a visa on arrival. However, this requires prior application through the website https://visaonarrival.gov.eg/. Warning! Visa on arrival procedures may vary depending on political relations. Therefore, I strongly advise you to check the current status with the consulate before your trip.

Before the visa-on-arrival system, it was easy for Turkish citizens under 18 and over 45 holding ordinary passports to obtain visas. However, the visa application process for Turkish citizens between 18 and 45 holding ordinary passports was truly quite difficult. I've heard of many people who had to cancel their tickets after waiting a year (!) because they couldn't get a visa.

Visa applications for passports are submitted to the two Egyptian representations in Türkiye: the Embassy of the Arab Republic of Egypt in Ankara (applications are accepted based on region and not from all provinces) and the Consulate General of the Arab Republic of Egypt in Istanbul (applications can be made from any province in Türkiye). Appointments at the Consulate General of the Arab Republic of Egypt in Istanbul are made through the Facebook page “Consulate General of the Arab Republic of Egypt in Istanbul – Visa Department”. On this page, you click on the “make an appointment” section, select a date and time for your visa appointment. You also send a dated, barcoded residence certificate obtained through e-government to the message you receive via Messenger. Then, you go and submit your application on your scheduled appointment day.

Meanwhile, there is another exception: Turkish citizens traveling to the Sinai region are granted entry with a 15-day visa on arrival, without paying a visa fee. This visa on arrival allows for a 15-day stay in the Sinai region (Sharm El-Sheikh – Taba – Dahab – Nuweiba – Saint Catherine).

List of places to visit in Egypt

First of all, when touring Egypt, it's a good idea to be prepared for the persistent behavior of street vendors, as they can drive you crazy. In fact, this is a situation that has persisted for centuries and has been noted by many famous travelers who visited Egypt. In the 17th century, Evliya Çelebi reportedly stated that trade in Egypt was "forceful" and the vendors' attitudes were "oppressive." In the 19th century, Gustave Flaubert wrote that bargaining was sometimes "intimidating" and "frustrating." Also in the 19th century, Edward William Lane emphasized that Egyptians needed to approach bargaining with "burning patience," observing that vendors were both "generous" and "stubborn." Richard Francis Burton, also in the 19th century, wrote that vendors in Egypt displayed a "patient" and sometimes "returning to the beginning" attitude during bargaining. Mark Twain, in the 19th century, humorously described vendors as "obsessively" demanding high prices and that bargaining had become "an art." In the 20th century, Gertrude Bell, while shopping in Egypt, emphasized that merchants displayed a "stubborn" and "direct" attitude, but that this was part of Egypt's historical and cultural fabric. All these observations reveal that trade in Egypt was not merely an economic activity, but also a social ritual and cultural norm.

Now let's take a look at the list of places to visit in Egypt…
Cairo

The capital city, Cairo , is the lifeblood of the country, housing numerous universities, colleges, theaters, museums, and monuments. The Cairo Egyptian Museum, the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization, the Grand Egyptian Museum, the Pyramids area, and the Nile River region are among the unique places popular with tourists. Memphis and its Necropolis – the Pyramid Sites from Giza to Dahshur – are UNESCO World Heritage sites. Medieval Cairo is also a UNESCO World Heritage site. Furthermore, Cairo is included in the UNESCO Creative Cities Network for crafts and folk arts.

Cairo, home to the pyramids, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World , and where swallowing the desert sand in the air is inevitable as you wander its streets, is a capital city where historical richness and chaos go hand in hand. Standing in the same place with the same name for over 1,300 years, Cairo is a haphazard blend of East and West, old and new.

al-Minya

Amenhotep IV (Akhenaton) founded the city of Tel el-Amarna, located in what is now the El-Minya governorate, around 1330 BC. Therefore, Tel el-Amarna is known as the city of Akhenaton and Nefertiti. Akhenaton created the monotheistic religion of Aten and declared Tel el-Amarna as its capital.

Sevhac (Sohag)

The Abydos settlement in Al-Balyana is located in the southwest of the Sohag (Arabic: Sohag) governorate. The Temple of Abydos here was built by Seti I and his son Ramses II. The temple was dedicated to seven gods, primarily Osiris, the God of the Underworld. Over time, it became an important religious center in Ancient Egypt and a pilgrimage site for the worship of Osiris. Even today, Egyptians believe that Abydos possesses fertility energy and visit it before marriage.
Temple of Abydos – Temple of Seti I
Kina (Qena)

Qena (Arabic: Qena) was the capital of the Qena Governorate. The Dendera Temple Complex here is one of the most important artifacts of ancient Egyptian civilization. The complex contains three temples: the Temple of the Nativity, the Temple of Isis, and the main temple dedicated to Hathor. Hathor was the ancient Egyptian goddess of love, fertility, music, and dance.
Dendera Temple Complex
Luxor

Luxor, the capital of the Luxor Governorate, is one of the first places to visit to experience thousands of years of history. The city was given its name, meaning "jewel," by the Arabs. Known as Thebes to the ancient Greeks , Luxor is built on the site of the ancient Egyptian city of Thebes, making it one of the world's largest open-air museums. Ancient Thebes and its necropolis are on the UNESCO World Heritage list.
Karnak Temple, Luxor
Assuan (Aswan)

Aswan is one of the leading cities in cultural Egyptian tourism routes. Its inclusion in the UNESCO Creative Cities Network for crafts and folk arts is also highly significant. The city reflects approximately 5,000 years of Nubian culture and history. All Nubian monuments, from Abu Simbel to the Temple of Philae in central Aswan, are on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

Abu Simbel

Abu Simbel , home to the Temple of Ramses II and the Temple of Nefertari, is a place that successfully reflects the mystery of Egypt through these two temples. The larger Temple of Ramses II is dedicated to Ramses II and his alleged victory against the Hittites in the Battle of Kadesh. The second temple, next to the Temple of Ramses II, is the Temple of Nefertari, dedicated to Ramses II's wife Nefertari and the fertility goddess Hathor. Two of the six 11.5-meter-tall statues in front of the temple belong to Nefertari.

Western Desert

The Western Desert is a Sahara region stretching from west of the Nile River to the Libyan border, and from the Mediterranean Sea south to the Sudanese border. Here, in the White Desert and Black Desert regions, visitors stay in Bedouin tents and go on desert safaris.

Alexandria

Located on the western edge of the Nile Delta , Alexandria is situated at the convergence of routes connecting Asia, Africa, and Europe. Today, Alexandria is a major tourism, trade, and transportation hub included in many Egyptian tour itineraries. It is renowned for the Lighthouse of Alexandria, one of the Seven Wonders of Antiquity, and the Library of Alexandria, the largest of its time. Surrounded by the magnificent shores of the Mediterranean, Alexandria offers visitors unforgettable experiences with its nearby charming seaside cities such as Rosetta and Marsa Matruh, and resorts like the Marina and Almaza Bay.

Abu Mena, located about 50 km southwest of Alexandria, was a town, monastic complex, and Christian pilgrimage center in Late Antique Egypt. Very few remains survive, but it is significant due to its role in early Christianity. Abu Mena is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
Sharm el Sheikh

Sharm El Sheikh , or simply Sharm, is a resort town on the Red Sea coast of the Sinai Peninsula. Also known as the "City of Peace," Sharm El Sheikh is a popular tourist destination for vacationers and divers, boasting golden sandy beaches, crystal-clear waters, and rich underwater life. Whether you want to explore the underwater riches in some of the world's most beautiful diving spots or enjoy ATV tours in the Sinai Desert, Sharm El Sheikh will delight you with its activities and natural beauty. The Ras Mohammed National Park here is on the UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List.

Hurghada

Hurghada , a city on the Red Sea coast of Egypt , boasts pristine blue waters. It also offers exciting safaris, allowing visitors to closely observe desert and Bedouin life. In Hurghada, you can fully enjoy a beach holiday, sun, and sand, or if you're interested in the underwater world, explore the rich underwater experience with a liveaboard (overnight scuba diving trip).

South Sinai

The Monastery of St. Catherine (also known as the Holy God-Trodden Monastery) is located in the South Sinai Governorate, at the foot of Mount Sinai. This Greek Orthodox monastery is one of the oldest monasteries open for worship in the world. Byzantine Emperor Justinian I had it built between 548 and 565 AD to house monks who had been living in the Sinai Peninsula since the 4th century. The well, the burning bush (a metaphorical representation of the enslavement of the Hebrews but their unwavering survival despite being burned), and the golden calf carved into a part of the mountain are all highly spiritual places. Those who wish can climb Mount Sinai at 2:00 AM to see the place where Moses spoke with God and witness an extraordinary sunrise. The Monastery of St. Catherine is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
